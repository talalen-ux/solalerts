Double-headers · same team twice
One team to win both its games this week, such as a European night and then the league. Tested over two seasons: favourites playing 2-4 days after a win won as often as predicted, so the two legs are priced separately.
Wildcards · long shots
Each wildcard takes the most likely result from 10, 15 or 19 different matches. Every leg is around 90%, yet one miss sinks the slip. At a typical 5% bookmaker margin per leg you get back about 61p, 48p and 40p per £1 staked on average. Treat them as a small-stake bit of fun, not a plan.

What the research says
Every leg multiplies the margin
Expected return on N legs is (1 − margin)N. New Jersey sportsbooks kept 19.2% of parlay stakes in 2025 against 4.9% on other bets (Whelan 2026).
How many legs
Even with an edge: singles below 33% per leg, two legs up to 53%, five legs only from 70%, ten from 85%. Each slip here is checked against that rule.
Only legs worth betting alone
In the optimal set of bets, a parlay belongs only if each leg has an edge on its own, and dropping parlays for singles costs almost nothing (Long 2026; Grant, Johnstone & Kwon 2008).
Near misses are normal
On a long slip, missing by one leg is several times more likely than winning. It says nothing about next time.
Before you bet
Fair odds are not value
The model is calibrated, so a slip at its fair price only breaks even.
Check line-ups
Team news is applied only where it could be sourced.
